Polybius Workshop

πραγματικὴ ἱστορία

A forty-week path from low-intermediate Greek to reading Polybius, with drills for vocabulary, parsing, syntax and composition, and printable sheets for pen-and-paper work.

The goal

Read Polybius with a lexicon by week 16, and with only occasional lookups by week 40

Plan on 45–60 minutes a day, six days a week. Fifteen minutes of that happens on your phone (vocabulary and quick drills). The rest happens at a desk with the text, a notebook and a pen. Composition runs alongside reading throughout, because writing Greek is the fastest way to make endings and constructions automatic.

What makes Polybius hard, and what to drill for it

Long periods built from participles and infinitives.Sentences of 60+ words are normal. Drill: colometry in your notebook, the Syntax tab, participle forms in Parsing.ἀναζεύξας … παραγενόμενος … συνιδὼν … ἐποιεῖτο τὴν ἔφοδον
Articular infinitives everywhere.Especially after prepositions: διὰ τό (because), ἐν τῷ (while), πρὸς τό (so that), ἅμα τῷ (as soon as).διὰ τὸ μὴ γινώσκειν τοὺς τόπους
Periphrasis with ποιέομαι + abstract noun.Polybius prefers "make the march" to "march". Learn the noun and you have the verb.ἐποιεῖτο τὴν πορείαν · ποιεῖσθαι τὴν ἀναχώρησιν
Abstract, Hellenistic vocabulary.Words in -σις, -μα, -ία and Polybian senses of common words. The Historian's toolkit deck targets these.διάληψις · ἐπιβολή · προαίρεσις · χειρισμός
Koine spelling and forms.γίνομαι and γινώσκω for γίγνομαι/γιγνώσκω; passive aorist ἐγενήθη beside ἐγένετο; ἱππεῖς as an accusative.ἐγενήθη = ἐγένετο
Word order bent to avoid hiatus.Polybius avoids a vowel-ending word before a vowel-initial one, so words land in odd places. Read by grammatical agreement, not position.ὑπὸ μίαν ἀρχὴν ἔπεσε τὴν Ῥωμαίων
Authorial cross-references and stock transitions.Recognize them instantly and you save a lookup in every paragraph.καθάπερ ἐπάνω προεδηλώσαμεν · κατὰ τὸν αὐτὸν καιρόν

Four phases

1
Weeks 1–6 · Consolidate the machinery
  • Morphology to automatic. Principal parts of the 30 verbs in the Principal parts deck. Every aorist form of τίθημι, ἵστημι, δίδωμι, ἵημι, δείκνυμι. Third declension in -σις, -ευς, -ος/-ους.
  • Vocabulary. Decks: Historian's toolkit, Verbs & idioms, Connectives, Principal parts. 10 new cards a day.
  • Bridge reading. Xenophon, Anabasis 1: armies, camps, marches and battles in simpler prose, two pages a week, fully parsed in your notebook.
  • Composition. Guided sentences, tier 1. A synopsis sheet twice a week.
  • Exit check. 90% or better on 20 parsing items; a synopsis of τίθημι from memory with no more than three errors.
2
Weeks 7–16 · First Polybius, fully supported
  • Reading, in this order. 1.20–1.23 (Rome builds a fleet; the κόραξ boarding bridge) → 3.47–3.56 (Hannibal crosses the Alps) → 1.1–1.5 (the preface, the hardest part, saved until now). 15–20 lines a session.
  • Method. The three-pass method below. Look at a translation only after your own written attempt.
  • Vocabulary. Add Army & war, Terrain & march, Peoples & places.
  • Syntax. Syntax drill daily (5 min). One colometry sheet a week on a preface sentence.
  • Composition. Tier 2 sentences, 3 per session, twice a week.
  • Exit check. A fresh 10 lines of Book 3 in 30 minutes with a lexicon, with a written translation you could defend.
3
Weeks 17–30 · Range and speed
  • Reading. 3.6–3.15 (causes of the Hannibalic War: αἰτία, πρόφασις, ἀρχή; Saguntum) → 3.107–3.117 (Cannae) → 6.11–6.18 (the Roman constitution) → selections from 6.19–6.42 (army and camp) → 29.27 (Popillius draws a circle around Antiochus).
  • Method shift. First pass fast with no writing, marking only what stops you. Then a focused second pass on the marked spots.
  • Vocabulary. Add State & diplomacy and Qualities & judgments. Start a personal word list in your notebook from your reading.
  • Composition. Tier 3 sentences, and a weekly back-translation of one Polybius paragraph.
  • Exit check. One Loeb page of Greek in 40 minutes.
4
Weeks 31–40 and beyond · Reading for content
  • Reading. A whole book (1 or 3) straight through. Then 10.2–10.20 (Scipio takes New Carthage), 31.23–31.30 (Polybius and Scipio Aemilianus), 38.21–38.22 (Scipio at the fall of Carthage), and Book 12 on how history should be written.
  • Vocabulary. Keep reviews to 10 minutes. New cards only from your own reading.
  • Composition. One 150-word paragraph a month in Polybian style. For example, narrate a modern event with his transitions and periphrases.
  • Exit check. Two Loeb pages an hour with occasional lookups.

The three-pass reading method

Pass 1: parse.Read the passage aloud once. Then, in your notebook, list every finite verb and participle with a full parse and lemma. Look up only what you can't parse.
Pass 2: translate.Write a literal translation, keeping clause boundaries visible. Then compare with the Loeb or Shuckburgh and mark each disagreement in the margin: V (vocabulary), M (morphology), S (syntax).
Pass 3: reread.Two days later, read the Greek aloud with no notes. Any phrase that still stops you goes into the phrasebook or onto a card.

Paper exercises (printouts or a lined notebook)

Every exercise marked print can be generated on the Worksheets tab. The rest need only a notebook.

Paradigm from memory notebookWrite a full paradigm (for example the aorist middle of τίθημι) from memory. Check it against a grammar and correct in red. Tomorrow, rewrite only the cells you got wrong.
Verb synopsis printOne verb, one person and number, every tense, mood and voice. The single best check on whether a verb's system is in your head.
Principal-parts ladder printThe present is given; write the other five parts. Cover the answer key with the folded sheet.
Colometry of a period printCopy a long Polybian sentence one clause per line, indenting each dependent clause under what it depends on. Box finite verbs, circle participles, underline infinitives, and draw an arrow from each relative pronoun to its antecedent. This is the core skill for Polybius.
Polybian phrasebook notebookReserve the back pages of your notebook. Formula on the left page, gloss and reference on the right. Start with: κατὰ τὸν αὐτὸν καιρόν · ὡς ἐπάνω διεσαφήσαμεν · ποιεῖσθαι τὴν πορείαν · ἐγκρατὴς γίνεσθαι + gen. · ἐπὶ τοσοῦτον … ὥστε · οὐ μὴν ἀλλά.
Copywork with accents notebookCopy five lines of the passage you are reading, accents and breathings included, then read them aloud. It slows your eye down to the level of endings and fixes accent patterns.
Fold-sheet vocabulary printGreek on the left, English on the right. Fold the sheet down the dashed line and test yourself both ways. Tick the ones you missed and add them to your phrasebook.
Back-translation printMonday: translate 6–8 lines of Polybius into English. Thursday: translate your English back into Greek without looking. Then compare and mark differences in three colors: vocabulary, morphology, word order/idiom.
Parsing grids and declension grids printQuick 10-minute warm-ups before a reading session.

Composition track

Tier 1 (weeks 1–6): correct forms in short sentences. Tier 2 (weeks 7–16): one Polybian construction per sentence (genitive absolute, articular infinitive, ποιέομαι periphrasis, result and purpose). Tier 3 (weeks 17+): full periods and back-translation of real passages. For more sentences, work through North & Hillard, Greek Prose Composition (1898), selecting the exercises on participles, infinitives, and conditions. It is old and Attic, but its drills transfer directly.

Resources

TextBüttner-Wobst Greek text with Shuckburgh's translation on Scaife Viewer / Perseus (free). Loeb Polybius, tr. Paton, rev. Walbank & Habicht (6 vols.), for facing-page work.
LexiconLogeion (LSJ and others; there is a phone app). Mauersberger, Polybios-Lexikon, for Polybian senses if you read German.
GrammarSmyth, Greek Grammar, for constructions. Goodwin, Syntax of the Moods and Tenses, for conditions and indirect discourse.
CommentaryWalbank, A Historical Commentary on Polybius (3 vols.): mostly historical, but it explains many difficult sentences.
Core vocabDickinson College Commentaries' Greek Core Vocabulary (≈500 most common words). Keep it in your rotation if anything there is shaky.
